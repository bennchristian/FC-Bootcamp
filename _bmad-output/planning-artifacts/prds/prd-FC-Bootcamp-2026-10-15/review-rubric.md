# PRD Quality Review — FC-Bootcamp

Calibration: solo, ~10-hour fellowship exercise, hard stop Fri 2026-10-16 17:30. §4.4 is an intentional TODO and is not counted as a finding; it is assessed only for whether the surrounding PRD is safe to build from without it (see Downstream usability).

## Overall verdict

This is a strong PRD for its stakes. The vision is concrete, the non-goals and cut order are explicit, most FRs carry a testable consequence, and the schedule arithmetic is sound with real buffer (about 4h45m of Friday slack against 10h of work). It is safe to start the portfolio now, and the §4.4 gating (FR-5, then a `bmad-prd` update, then FR-6) is sequenced correctly. Two things are at risk. First, half of the stated purpose, showing Justin Ben's product sense, has no deliverable in the hand-off and no metric. Second, the repo's `AGENTS.md`, `CLAUDE.md` and root `PRD.md` still describe the old scope (Sat 02:00 deadline, broken-deploy exercise, reflection video), and an implementer who reads them first will be steered wrong. Neither is expensive to fix.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Decisions are stated as decisions and name what was given up. Examples are §6.2 ("three features that work beat six that half work"), the never-cut set (FR-12, FR-15, FR-16), and the Non-goals list. The hard stop is justified, and Sunday is explicitly buffer rather than plan. What holds it back from strong is three items that are decided late, decided by an owner only, or not decided at all. One of them has a decision window of about 35 minutes from now.

### Findings
- **medium** Open Question 2 has no default and collides with a repo rule (§9 Q2; RULES.md Process rules) — The question is "Where do the `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-ux` runs fit?", with an answer due "before the portfolio scaffold starts" (Thu 15:15). `RULES.md` already says "Before any implementation, the eight context files exist and are current". The root `ARCHITECTURE.md`, `DESIGN.md` and `SCHEMA.md` are stubs, and `SCHEMA.md` cannot be filled until §4.4. The PRD neither proposes a default nor says whether the stubs satisfy the rule. *Fix:* record a default, for example "root stubs count as existing for the portfolio phase; ARCHITECTURE/UX/SCHEMA runs happen after §4.4 and come out of the Fri 12:00–15:30 buffer". Add one line saying `RULES.md` is being relaxed on purpose.
- **medium** The walkthrough-for-reflection substitution is stated as settled but is only the owner's decision (§1, §5 "A reflection video. It's deferred…", Addendum B) — Per `CLAUDE.md` §1 and the memlog, Justin's own brief asked for a reflection video. Ben replaced it with the walkthrough video. Nothing records that Justin accepts this, and it is not tagged `[ASSUMPTION]` (the only related tag covers the hard-part disclosure). The success condition is what Justin receives, so this is the riskiest unconfirmed premise in the PRD. *Fix:* tag it `[ASSUMPTION: Justin accepts the walkthrough video in place of the reflection video]` and index it in §10, or add a one-line confirmation to Open Questions with a cheap fallback.
- **low** The FR-12 reset pattern has no owner, time or default, and the Glossary contradicts the addendum (FR-12; §3 "Demo login"; Addendum A) — FR-12 says "decided before building it". The addendum shows two patterns and favours the per-visitor throwaway account. The Glossary defines Demo login as signing in to "a shared demo account", which already rules the throwaway pattern out. FR-12 and SM-3 also presume a delete action ("a visitor deletes everything") that no FR requires. *Fix:* make the Glossary pattern-neutral ("a demo account"), name the decision point (before the FR-12 build, around Fri 10:30), and state a default so the decision isn't re-opened under time pressure.

## Substance over theater — strong

There are no personas, only two jobs to be done, and both drive real decisions. There is no innovation section. The NFR-style requirements are product-specific rather than boilerplate: 375px width, RLS plus an explicit policy "in the same change that creates it", keys never committed, and a private-window test. The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD, because it names the date, the reviewer, the three outputs and the bar. Counter-metrics SM-C1 and SM-C2 are tied to specific budget decisions rather than added for decoration. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is clear: Justin should be able to see in a few minutes that Ben can ship a real product end to end, and judge his scope thinking. Feature prioritization follows from it, with a clone that works on real data, a demo login, and a cut order that protects what Justin sees. The scope kind (problem-solving/experience hybrid, with polish capped) matches the logic in §6.

### Findings
- **high** The "product sense" half of the thesis has no deliverable and no metric (§2.1 Jobs to be done; FR-6; FR-15; FR-16; §7) — Justin's job is to judge "how he thinks about scope", and Ben's job is to "show product sense through the breakdown and the scope check". But FR-16's hand-off list (portfolio link, clone link, repo link, demo login instructions, video link) does not include the breakdown doc or the scope check. FR-6 says only that the scope check "exists before the first `clone:` commit" and never says where it lives (the memlog's "posted in the Space" assumption was dropped). FR-15 requires the video only to name the hard part. That is 65 minutes (about 11% of the budget) producing artifacts the reviewer may never see. SM-1 to SM-3 all measure delivery and function, none measure product sense. *Fix:* add the breakdown doc link and the scope-check location to FR-16, or require 20–30 seconds on them in FR-15. State where the scope check is written in FR-6.
- **low** The Vision overclaims against the addendum (§1 "meets the bar set by Justin's reference examples"; Addendum A "What separates the polished ones"; §6.2) — Addendum A says the polished references differ by end-to-end uploads, several real users, a bottom tab bar and a second feature layer. §6.2 deliberately excludes the second layer and the real hard part. The actual target is roughly Justin's own YouTube clone plus a demo login, which §1's second half implicitly says. *Fix:* name the reference level, for example "matches Justin's YouTube clone and adds a demo login".
- **low** The cut order has no triggers (§6.2 "Cut order if the schedule slips"; §8 hour-by-hour) — Without a checkpoint, "slips" is decided by feel at the moment it hurts. The schedule already has natural checkpoints (Thu 22:35 end of steps 1–4, Fri 12:00 end of polish). FR-3 is last in the cut order but is scheduled Thu 16:15–17:45, so it is either already done or already late before the clone build starts. *Fix:* tie each cut to a checkpoint, for example "if steps 1–4 aren't done by Thu 22:35, FR-14 is cut".

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the PRD's best-structured dimension. Nearly every FR has a "Consequences (testable)" block with verifiable conditions: "loads in a private window", "one commit per build-order step, prefixed `clone:`", "every table has RLS enabled", "survives a refresh and closing the browser". The soft spots are a handful of adjectives, one assumption baked into the second-account test, and one unspecified auth detail that can silently break the headline success metric.

### Findings
- **medium** The second-account test assumes user-owned data (FR-10; UJ-3; §7 SM-2) — UJ-3 says the second user "sees their own data rather than the demo account's", and FR-10 requires the test to pass "for every feature". Per Addendum A, two of the three reference clones show public content to signed-out visitors. If the chosen product's core feature is a shared feed or catalogue, "their own data rather than the demo account's" is false by design and "every feature" is untestable. The surrounding FRs are otherwise safe to build from without §4.4, but this one is not product-agnostic. *Fix:* reword the test now, for example "content the second user creates appears for them and persists; they cannot edit or delete another user's content; private data (if any) is not visible across accounts". Alternatively re-check the wording when §4.4 is filled.
- **medium** FR-8 doesn't say how accounts work or how email confirmation is handled (FR-8; UJ-3; SM-2; FR-12) — It says "sign up, sign in and sign out" but gives no method or confirmation policy. If Supabase email confirmation stays on (commonly the default for new projects), a different-browser sign-up in UJ-3 and a throwaway demo account in FR-12 can both stall on an inbox, or on email rate limits. Addendum C lists the "most common" blockers but omits this. It is a Ben-owned dashboard setting, so it should be an explicit step. *Fix:* specify email plus password in FR-8, decide confirmation on or off for this run, list it as a Ben dashboard step, and add it to Addendum C.
- **medium** Soft or unbounded acceptance wording (FR-13; §4.7 "Phone"; FR-15; FR-4) — Quoted phrases: "don't look broken" (empty states); "usable at phone width (375px)"; "Runs about three minutes"; "could realistically be rebuilt in the clone build budget". FR-13's checks are the best of the lot (side-by-side, on live link), but "broken" and "usable" are judgment calls. *Fix:* give each a bound. For phone: no horizontal scroll at 375px and every primary action reachable. For empty states: each empty list shows an instruction or a call to action. For the video: 2:30–3:30. For FR-4: "realistic" means it passes the FR-6 test of 3–4 tables and a named hard-part substitute.
- **low** The FR-6 table count is ambiguous (FR-6 "three or four tables"; FR-8 "creates the user's `profiles` row") — `profiles` is required, so it is unclear whether it counts toward the three or four. That swings the domain-table budget from 2–3 to 3–4, in the one place the PRD says scope gets cut. *Fix:* "three or four tables, `profiles` included (or excluded)".
- **low** The ordering rules don't line up with the build order (FR-7 "One commit per build-order step"; FR-8 "Accounts exist before anything writes to the database"; Glossary "Build order") — The build order lists accounts as step 4, after tables (2) and the core feature (3), where hand-typed rows are written, which `RULES.md` permits. Steps 7 ("redeploy after each step") and 8 ("second-account test") don't produce commits on their own. *Fix:* "before any app code writes" in FR-8, and "one commit per step that changes code" in FR-7.

## Scope honesty — strong

Non-goals are explicit and each is confirmed in the memlog: Week 4 artifacts, the broken-deploy exercise, the reflection video, Lovable, extra libraries, a YouTube clone, a portfolio backend, a custom domain, and programme deliverables 3 and 4. De-scoping is stated openly rather than done silently, and the schedule inferences are tagged. The Assumptions Index round-trips (8 inline, 8 indexed). Open-items density (2 open questions, 8 assumptions in about 3,000 words) is appropriate for the stakes. The only gaps are the unconfirmed Justin premise (Decision-readiness) and the missing cut triggers (Strategic coherence), both already listed above. No separate findings.

## Downstream usability — adequate

The PRD is built to feed `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-ux`, and the Glossary plus FR/UJ/SM IDs make that easy. The one substantive problem is outside the PRD file, in the docs a cold implementer reads before it.

### Findings
- **high** Repo docs that an implementer reads first contradict the PRD, and nothing in the PRD closes the loop (§0; `AGENTS.md` Phase gating; `CLAUDE.md` §1; root `PRD.md` stub) — `AGENTS.md` still gives the deadline as "Sat 2026-10-17 02:00", lists "A deliberate broken deploy" under Phase 3, and ends Ship with "Record the reflection video". `CLAUDE.md` §1 says the third output is the reflection video, and its time frame is 36 hours. The root `PRD.md` is still the stub (reflection video, 36-hour run), and the promotion of this run's output to root is not a step in the PRD or the schedule. The global rules say to read `AGENTS.md` before starting work. An agent pacing itself to 02:00 and phase-gating on the old list would widen scope against the PRD, which is exactly the failure the PRD exists to prevent. *Fix:* add a "Reconcile before building" item to the plan. Promote this PRD to root `PRD.md`, then update `AGENTS.md` phase gating and `CLAUDE.md` §1 (deadline, outputs, budget). Fold the PRD §0 pointer into that one step.
- **low** The addendum's FR-3 candidate conflicts with the schedule (Addendum A "including the clone once it's live"; §8) — FR-3 is scheduled Thu 16:15–17:45, but the clone is not a presentable thing to write up until Fri. As written, that candidate can't be one of the five unless FR-3 slips later. Nothing in the PRD links the portfolio to the clone at all. *Fix:* drop the candidate, or schedule one portfolio change after Fri 12:00.

## Shape fit — adequate

For a solo, deadline-bound exercise, the shape is right: a scope contract with a schedule, four short journeys with a named reviewer, capability FRs with consequences, and no persona or market sections. UJs earn their place for UJ-2 and UJ-3, where the product flows are real, and cost little elsewhere. The cost is length and mixed content.

### Findings
- **low** The PRD is about 3,150 words, roughly three times the intended two pages, and partly restates other repo docs (§4.7; FR-7; FR-9; FR-13) — Stack rules, "keys never committed", RLS-with-policy, the commit-prefix rule and the demo-reset rule already live in `RULES.md` and `AGENTS.md`. Keeping them in both places creates a drift risk, which the high finding on stale repo docs shows is not hypothetical. The §8 table and the hour-by-hour table also overlap. *Fix:* keep the testable consequence in the FR and cut the rationale, or reference `RULES.md` instead of restating it. Collapse §8's two tables if time is short.
- **low** Process tasks and product capabilities share one FR sequence (FR-1 to FR-7, FR-11, FR-15, FR-16 versus FR-8 to FR-10, FR-12 to FR-14) — Nine of the sixteen FRs are things Ben does (write a scope check, list what's half-working, send a message), not behaviours of the product. `bmad-ux` and `bmad-architecture` will source-extract from FR-8 to FR-10, FR-12 to FR-14 and §4.4, and treat the rest as noise. *Fix:* a one-line note in §0 or a "Deliverable" tag on the process FRs is enough.
